# Historical data editorial standards

## Scope
This project provides a global, non-teleological history of human societies. It does not assume every society progresses through the same stages or that European historical period labels apply worldwide.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. Dated archaeological evidence, primary historical documents and peer-reviewed syntheses.
2. Curated institutional collections with specific object, excavation or historical records.
3. General educational reference works for initial discovery only.

## Required future event schema
Each entry should eventually have: stable ID, display title, start and end dates (including precision and calendar convention), geography with uncertainty, thematic tags, concise summary, explanatory context, competing interpretations where relevant, source URLs with authors and publication dates, editorial reviewer, and last-reviewed date.

## Existing date labels
- **documented**: conventionally dated historical event, not a claim of perfect historical certainty.
- **approximate**: approximate time anchor, often representing long processes.
- **traditional**: date reported in historical tradition and not necessarily independently confirmed.

These are *not* source-confidence scores. All current entries require individual citation review.

## Known historiographic risks
- Avoid implying that farming, cities, writing, industrialization or democracy represent universal or inevitable progress.
- Include histories of Indigenous peoples and non-state societies without treating absence of a centralized state as absence of history.
- Do not equate the presence of a civilization with a modern ethnic or national identity.
- Do not treat imperial borders as stable across centuries.
- Do not assign single-year precision to migrations, technological diffusion, social changes or archaeological cultures.
- Use 'BCE/CE' for display and note that historical chronology has no year zero in conventional BCE/CE notation.
- Account for dates that vary across scholarly traditions.

## Research starting points
- Smithsonian Human Origins Program: https://humanorigins.si.edu/
- Metropolitan Museum Timeline of Art History: https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/
- British Museum Collection: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/
- World History Encyclopedia (secondary introduction): https://www.worldhistory.org/
- Our World in Data: https://ourworldindata.org/

These are not direct event-level citations. Do not represent them as proof of any specific entry without source review.

## Roadmap
1. Attach specific sources to each record and distinguish archaeological, textual and interpretive claims.
2. Represent uncertainty intervals and multi-century processes explicitly.
3. Add global regional editorial coverage checks.
4. Implement georeferenced, time-aware spatial layers from appropriately licensed datasets.
5. Use quantitative historical series only with documented measurement methods and uncertainty.
